## What you see

1. Question: on the left.
2. Full answer: on the left, with the sentence to label marked.
3. Passages cited: on the left, one panel per cited passage.
4. Sentence to label: on the right, beside the choices.

## Choices

Pick one choice for each item.

### Factual claim (`factual`)

Rule: The sentence states something that can be checked against a source. A negative answer that answers the question counts ("No debt securities are listed").

Worked example (label Factual claim): Sentence: "The company was founded in 1998 and has 300 staff."

### Refusal (`refusal`)

Rule: The sentence only says that the sources do not cover something. It states no checkable fact, not even about what a source contains.

Worked example (label Refusal): Sentence: "The provided sources do not cover the 2019 audit."

### Mixed (`mixed`)

Rule: The sentence states a checkable fact and also says that the sources lack the answer or part of it. Mixed counts as factual in scoring.

Worked example (label Mixed): Sentence: "The policy page lists three plans but gives no prices."

### Can't tell (`cant_tell`)

Rule: Use this rarely. Choose it only when the sentence is too unclear to sort.

Worked example (label Can't tell): Sentence: "As noted above." (a fragment you cannot sort)

The worked examples are made up for this guideline. None comes from a dataset, so none is in a sample.

## Hard cases

- A sentence that says what a source contains and also says that the source lacks the answer is Mixed, not Refusal. Example: "Source X lists subsidiaries but no debt securities" is Mixed, because the list of subsidiaries is a checkable fact about the source.
- A negative answer that answers the question is Factual. Example: "No debt securities are listed."
- Refusal needs no checkable fact at all, not even about what a source contains.
- Mixed counts as factual in scoring. Choose Mixed when it fits.

## Registered rules

Each rule below is copied word for word from the file named after it.

- The labeller sees the question, the full answer with the claim marked, and the passages the claim cites. (`preregistration.md`)
- Claim-kind rule (owner decision 2026-11-23, pre-freeze, from a practice labelling round): a sentence that states a checkable fact about what a source contains and also says that the source lacks the answer is mixed, not a refusal. A refusal states no checkable fact, not even about what a source contains. (`preregistration.md`)
- A negative finding that answers the question (for example, "no debt securities are listed") is a factual claim. (`protocol.md`)
- Agreement is refusal against not refusal (fact and mixed both count as not refusal). (`preregistration.md`)
- "Can't tell" labels are left out and counted. (`preregistration.md`)
- Labellers never see scores, verdicts, setup names or chunk sizes. (`preregistration.md`)
- Cited passage length can still hint at chunk size; the report says so. (`preregistration.md`)
- Disagreement rule: L1 and L2 discuss; if they cannot agree, the item is "can't tell" and counted. (`preregistration.md`)
- A training round on practice items comes first and is not evidence. (`preregistration.md`)
